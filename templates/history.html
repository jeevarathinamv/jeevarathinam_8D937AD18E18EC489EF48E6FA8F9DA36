Imperial Biography

Chronicles of Raja Raja Cholan

From his illustrious birth in Tirukoilur to the golden epoch of coronation and the serene final days of a monarch who reshaped Southern Asian history.

947 CE

Birth & Royal Lineage

Born on the auspicious day of Sadhayam star in the Tamil month of Aippasi at Tirukoilur, he was named Arulmozhivarman ("He whose words are graced by benevolence"). He was the third child of King Parantaka Sundara Chola and Queen Vanavan Mahadevi.

Malayaman & Chola Lineage
947 – 969 CE

Family & Mentorship of Kundavai

Growing alongside his elder brother Aditya Karikalan and elder sister Princess Kundavai Pirattiyar, Arulmozhi was mentored in statecraft, classical Tamil epics, Vedic philosophy, and warfare tactics. Kundavai remained his lifelong advisor and royal confidante.

Aditya Karikalan • Kundavai
969 – 985 CE

Selfless Magnanimity to Uttama Chola

Following the tragic assassination of Aditya Karikalan, popular clamor demanded that prince Arulmozhivarman ascend the throne. However, to maintain family harmony and avoid dynastic discord, Arulmozhi magnanimously insisted that his paternal uncle Uttama Chola reign first.

Unprecedented Statecraft
985 CE

Coronation as Rajakesari Arulmozhivarman

Following Uttama Chola's death in 985 CE, Arulmozhivarman ascended the imperial throne under the regnal title Rajakesari. Soon recognized for his boundless achievements, he assumed the everlasting title Raja Raja ("King of Kings").

Imperial Accession
985 – 1010 CE

Golden Epoch of Governance & Architecture

During his 30-year reign, Raja Raja implemented imperial land surveys, organized standing maritime fleets, salvaged the sacred Saivite Thevaram hymns, and culminated his devotion with the construction of the Thanjavur Big Temple (Peruvudaiyar Kovil).

Peruvudaiyar Kovil • Land Cadastre
1012 – 1014 CE

Co-Regency with Rajendra & Final Rest

In 1012 CE, Raja Raja appointed his battle-hardened son Rajendra Chola I as co-regent, ensuring an effortless transfer of power. The great emperor breathed his last in 1014 CE. His sacred memorial (Palli-padai) at Udayalur near Kumbakonam remains a place of reverence.

Udayalur Memorial • Rajendra I
